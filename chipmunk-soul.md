# Chipmunk soul

> How to make a chipmunk soul beat: choosing the vocal, how far to pitch it up, why repitching beats pitch shifting, and the drums that go under it.

Somebody put a 1971 soul record on at the wrong speed, heard a woman turn into a bird, and instead of fixing it built a sixteen-bar loop out of it. Two decades of radio followed.

Chipmunk soul is hip hop built on soul and gospel vocals sped up or pitched up several semitones, so the singer sounds smaller, brighter and faster than the record. You make one by finding a vocal phrase with air around it, pitching it up three to seven semitones, chopping it into a new arrangement, filtering the top and bottom so it sits, and putting hard drums underneath. The character comes from the formants moving with the pitch, which is what makes a grown singer sound like a cartoon.

## The short version

- **Source**: soul, gospel or disco vocal with space around it

- **Amount**: up 3–7 semitones, or 33 rpm material played at 45

- **Method**: repitch, so speed and pitch move together

- **Chop**: rearrange the phrase, do not loop the bar

- **Drums**: hard and simple: the sample is the melody and the hook

- **Clean up**: high-pass the sample, leave the low end to the kick

## Where it comes from

The technique is older than the nickname. Producers were speeding soul records up through the early nineties, partly for the sound and partly because sampling time was expensive and a shorter, faster sample cost less of it. What fixed it in the public ear was a run of Roc-A-Fella records at the turn of the century, above all Jay-Z's "The Blueprint" in 2001, where Kanye West and Just Blaze built most of an album out of sped-up soul vocals and drums that hit like a door closing.

"Chipmunk soul" started as a dig. It stuck because it is accurate: a voice moved up far enough stops being a person and becomes an instrument that no keyboard can imitate, still carrying the phrasing of a singer who meant it.

## How to make a chipmunk soul beat

- Find a vocal phrase two to four bars long with the band thin behind it.

- Repitch it up three to seven semitones, letting the tempo rise with the pitch.

- Set the project tempo to whatever the sped-up loop now lands on, often 85 to 100 bpm.

- Chop the phrase and play a new order, so the hook lands where your drums want it.

- High-pass the sample around 150 to 250 Hz so the kick owns the bottom.

- Take the top off above 10 kHz if the pitching made the sibilance sharp.

- Program drums that are simple and loud, and leave the sample alone during the fills.

- Play a bass line under the new key, not the old one.

## Pitch it the right way

Repitching, where the speed and the pitch move together, is the sound. A modern pitch shifter that holds the tempo will give you the note without the character, because the formants stay where they were and the singer stays adult. Formant shifting explains the difference, and varispeed covers the mechanism that ties speed and pitch together.

Two practical notes. Pitching up shortens the sample, so a four-bar phrase at +5 semitones is no longer four bars, and you either chop to fit or change your tempo. And pitching up raises the noise, the hiss and the room with everything else, which is usually a feature: it is where the record texture comes from once the drums arrive.

## Where it costs you

It is a recognizable sound with a date on it, and a whole beat in that style announces the era it belongs to. It is also unkind to sibilance: past about five semitones, S sounds turn into small explosions, and a de-esser before the pitching saves an afternoon. Clearance is the real cost, though. A pitched-up soul vocal is still someone's recording and someone's song, and speed does not disguise anything from the people whose job it is to notice. ## Questions people ask

### Did Kanye West invent chipmunk soul?

No. Pitched-up soul vocals were in hip hop through the nineties. His production with Just Blaze on Roc-A-Fella records at the start of the 2000s, particularly Jay-Z's "The Blueprint" in 2001, made it a signature sound and gave the nickname something to stick to.

### How many semitones should I pitch a soul sample up?

Three to seven is the usual range. Under three and it sounds like a mistake; past seven the voice turns brittle and the consonants break up. Let the key of the sped-up loop decide the key of the beat rather than the other way round.

### What BPM is chipmunk soul?

Most of it sits between 85 and 100 bpm, because that is where a soul record sped up by a few percent lands and where hard, simple drums have room to hit.

### Why does speeding up a voice make it sound like a chipmunk?

Speeding up a recording raises the pitch and the vocal tract resonances together. Your ear uses those resonances to judge the size of a speaker, so a uniformly raised voice reads as a much smaller person rather than the same person singing higher.
