## What a polysynth plugin actually is

A polysynth sounds several notes at once. Each key you hold takes a voice, and a voice is a complete signal path: oscillators, a filter, two envelopes, an amplifier. Hold a four-note chord and four of those paths run side by side.

There is a cost built into that. Every voice is arithmetic your computer has to do, so every polysynth has a ceiling and decides what happens when you reach it. That decision is called voice stealing, and it is why the chord in the opening paragraph vanished: the synth needed a voice, had none spare, and took the oldest one.

Where a monosynth spends everything on one note, a polysynth divides the same attention across however many you hold. Our piece on [monosynth against polysynth](https://gazillionindustries.com/monosynth-vs-polysynth/) works through what each gives up: one is for the line, the other for the harmony, and most sessions want both.

## What voice count changes in practice

Voice count decides one thing you can hear and several you cannot. What you hear is the handover. Play a chord, let it ring, play the next before the first has decayed, and the release tails of the old notes are still holding voices. Ten fingers across two chords with a three-second release is twenty voices for a moment, from ten keys.

Add the sustain pedal and the arithmetic runs away from you. A pedaled passage at 90 BPM with a four-second release can be holding thirty or forty voices before you notice, and the first sign is never a warning. It is a chord that ends early, inside the part you already liked.

WINNETKA's manual puts the ceiling at up to 128 voices at once in POLY on a single timbre, and 64 each when two POLY timbres are layered, which is the same 128 divided in half. At those numbers the count stops being something you manage. The four red lights on the board follow the voices as they sound, so you can watch the instrument work without reading a number.

### The other two voice modes, and what they are for

POLY is one of three settings on the VOICE row. MONO plays one note at a time, turning the same instrument into a single-line synth for a bass or a lead. UNISON stacks four voices on every note, two per timbre when you are layered, spread apart by a DETUNE control running from 0 to 99 cents.

UNISON is where detune stops being a garnish. Four voices five cents apart at 220 Hz beat at about 0.64 Hz, one slow swell every 1.6 seconds, and that reads as width. At 99 cents the beating passes 12 Hz and reads as a chorus you cannot switch off.

## Layering two timbres, and what that buys

LAYER runs two complete sounds from one set of keys. Each timbre carries its own full set of rows, VOICE through PATCH 4 — oscillators, filter, envelopes, LFOs and patch cords — while the effects and the arpeggiator are shared. TIMBRE 1 and TIMBRE 2 choose which one the knobs and the screen are showing.

The reason to reach for it is register. A pad that works in the middle of the keyboard thins out two octaves down and turns to mud two octaves up, and the fix is two sounds with different filter settings covering different parts of the range. One layer holds the body, the other the air, and one MIDI part plays both.

SYNC makes the mode usable rather than tedious. With SYNC lit, every edit lands on both timbres at once: knobs, panel keys and selectors, incoming MIDI controllers, even the envelope RESET key. Build one sound with SYNC on, turn it off, then move the second layer's filter and tuning away from the first. The bill is voices and attention, since two POLY timbres split the pool into 64 each and a patch with two filters takes twice as long to fix when it is nearly right.

## What separates a good polysynth from a spec sheet

Five things, and only two appear on a feature list. This is the checklist worth running before you spend anything, on any synth, ours included.

### Voices you never have to count

Hold a chord, play the next over the top with the pedal down, and listen to the first chord's tail. If it survives intact, the ceiling is above your playing. If it clips off, you have found the limit, and every pedaled passage you write will find it again.

### A filter that survives resonance

Take resonance to three quarters and sweep the cutoff across the range. Listen for the peak swamping the chord, for level jumping as it crosses a strong harmonic, and for the low end thinning as resonance rises. WINNETKA offers 24 LPF, 12 LPF, 12 BPF and 12 HPF, from 20 Hz to 20 kHz. A 24 dB low-pass at 500 Hz is 24 dB down at 1 kHz and 48 dB down at 2 kHz.

### Envelopes fast enough for stabs

Chords are not only pads. Set attack and decay to minimum, sustain to zero, and play a chord: a good envelope gives you a stab you can put on an offbeat, a slow one gives a soft blip whatever the numbers say. WINNETKA's F-ADSR and A-ADSR run 0 to 20 seconds a stage, and the manual is explicit that 0 is instant and 1 is a millisecond.

### Modulation you can reach in four moves

A patch cord that takes a submenu is a patch cord you will not make. WINNETKA gives you four, each sending a SOURCE to a DEST by an INT of ±63, and cords landing on the same destination add up. Sources run from the two envelopes and two LFOs through velocity, key track, pitch bend and the mod wheel; destinations cover pitch, OSC 2 tune, OSC 1's CONTROL 1, noise, cutoff, amp, pan and LFO 2's rate.

## How to audition a polysynth in ten minutes

Do this before you buy anything, in your own session, on your own speakers. Ten minutes gets you further than an hour of reading roundups, this one included.

- Load the synth on an empty track and open the first pad preset in the bank.

- Hold a four-note chord for eight bars with the sustain pedal down.

- Play a second four-note chord before releasing the first, and listen for the older notes cutting off.

- Sweep the filter cutoff from 20 Hz to 20 kHz once, slowly, with the chord held.

- Raise resonance to 75 percent and repeat the sweep.

- Set the amp envelope attack to 0 and decay to 200 ms, then play the same chord as stabs.

- Switch to unison, set detune to 5 cents, then to 50 cents, holding one note through each.

- Layer a bass timbre under the pad and play the bottom two octaves.

- Step through twenty presets with the arrow keys and count how many you would actually use.

- Bounce eight bars and play them back on a phone speaker and in the car.

Step three decides the purchase, and step ten is the one people skip. A pad that reads as enormous on monitors can arrive on a phone as a thin buzz, because a small speaker gives you almost nothing under 500 Hz and a chord's weight lives exactly there. The fix is upper harmonics rather than more level, and you can make it in the synth before it reaches the mix.

## The vocoder, and what it needs from you

A vocoder puts the shape of one sound onto another. WINNETKA listens on its audio input, measures what it hears across 16 bands, and applies that shape to the notes you play, so the chord under your hands speaks. It needs both halves: audio coming in, and keys held down.

Routing is the part that stops people. The left input channel is the voice it listens to; the right can carry an instrument to be played through the vocoder as well, raised with INST in the MIXER row. In Ableton Live, set your voice track's Audio To to the WINNETKA track and pick Audio In-WINNETKA underneath. The standalone app starts muted so it cannot feed back, so open Settings, choose your input, untick the mute, and wear headphones.

Twelve of the forty vocoder presets hold a vowel inside them and play with no audio at all, leaving 28 that want a microphone. You can hear what the vocoder does, and use it on a record, without plugging anything in. FORMANT then moves the held shape: SHIFT steps it down or up by up to two bands, CUTOFF slides it smoothly by up to two bands either way, RESONANCE sets how sharp the bands are, and HOLD freezes what is there now and saves it with your session. ## Polysynth or monosynth, and which to buy first

Buy for the part you cannot currently write. If your beats stall because the chords sound like a stock piano, a polysynth is the purchase. If they stall because the bass has no character, a monosynth is, and our guide to [choosing a monosynth VST](https://gazillionindustries.com/best-monosynth-vst/) is the page to read instead of this one.

The two instruments answer different questions. A monosynth asks what a single note should do, so it gives you glide that tracks the interval, note priority, trigger modes and a panel arranged for one line. A polysynth asks how several notes behave together, so it gives you voice assignment, unison and detune, and layering. JOURNEYMAN is our monosynth at $59; WINNETKA is the polysynth at $49.

The overlap is real and worth using. WINNETKA's MONO setting plays one note at a time with SINGLE and MULTI trigger modes and portamento from 2 ms to 5 seconds, so it writes a bass line perfectly well. What it does not carry is the note-priority behavior a monosynth is designed around, which matters on bass and barely registers on a lead.

## What a polysynth costs you

Every instrument sends a bill. This one arrives in four parts, and all four are worth knowing before you spend.

It costs CPU. Voices are arithmetic, and a pedaled passage holding forty of them costs roughly forty times one. Bounce the part to audio once the sound is right, and your session will still open in a year.

It costs decisions. WINNETKA gives each timbre one filter, two oscillators and four patch cords, and there is no reverb in the box — the shared chain runs mod FX, then delay, then EQ. Those are real limits, and they also mean a patch takes four moves rather than forty, which is the trade the instrument makes on purpose.

It costs arrangement space. A chord occupies a band of the spectrum rather than a line through it, so two polysynth parts in the same octave will fight for the whole record before an EQ gets a say. Give each a different filter setting and a different register.

It costs setup on the vocoder. Getting audio into a plug-in's own input is a routing job in every DAW, and it is five minutes the first time. The twelve held-vowel presets are the way around it while you decide whether the routing is worth learning.
