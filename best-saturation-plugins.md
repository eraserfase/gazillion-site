## Saturating a drum bus, start to finish

The technique matters more than the badge, so here it is with the cheap one. Put the saturator last on the drum bus, after whatever compression you are running and before anything on the master. Compress first so the saturator meets a steadier peak, then saturate so the harmonics land on a signal that already has its shape.

- Load BEEFY on the drum bus, after your compressor.

- Leave SOFT CLIP switched on. It handles the initial gain staging.

- Raise JUICE until the input clip light flickers on the loudest hits and goes dark between them.

- Turn BEEF up slowly until the loop stops growing and starts shrinking, then come back about a third.

- Move COOK toward the bright end until the snare reads on a phone speaker, then back off until the hats stop stinging.

- Bypass, match the two levels by ear, and judge the change on the worst speaker you own.

The cost is the same whichever plugin you use. You are trading crest factor for density, and past a point the kit stops sounding like a kit hitting and starts sounding like a wall standing still. Step six is the only real protection against that, and it is the step everybody skips. Level-matched or it did not happen. The long way round is in [how to saturate drums](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-saturate-drums/), and the arithmetic behind the density is in [crest factor](https://gazillionindustries.com/crest-factor/).

If what that bus actually wants is glue rather than harmonics, we have a free one and you should take it: [DRUGS](https://gazillionindustries.com/drugs.html) is a bus compressor for drums with one macro knob called DOSE, plus GAIN and MIX. Turning DOSE up makes a drum bus denser rather than louder. It costs nothing, so it also settles which of the two problems you have before you spend anything.

## FabFilter Saturn 2 — the opposite design

Multiband, where everything else here is broadband. Separate distortion style, drive, dynamics, feedback and tone per band, a modulation section with envelope followers, LFOs and MIDI sources that can drive almost any control, five creative FX styles alongside models FabFilter describes as inspired by tubes, tape, transformers and guitar amps, and linear-phase processing aimed at mastering. FabFilter does not print a price on the product page, so we are not going to invent one; there is a 30-day trial.

This is the right tool for one job and the wrong tool for the common one. Distorting only the 2 to 5 kHz band of a vocal while the low end stays clean is a thing Saturn 2 does and BEEFY cannot. Making a drum bus fatter in ten seconds is a thing you do not need six bands and a modulation matrix for, and the learning curve is the price you pay either way.

## Questions people ask

### What is the best saturation plugin?

For the broadband drum-bus job that most people are actually asking about, ours, at $19, with the clipping stage already in it. For per-band work, Saturn 2. For nothing at all, the Softube Saturation Knob. For speed and a name your engineer will recognize, Decapitator. Anybody who gives you one answer for all four of those is selling something.

### Is an expensive saturation plugin worth it?

It buys control and workflow, not a fundamentally different kind of distortion. If you use one band and one knob, you are paying for features you are not using. Work out which of those two people you are before the sale ends rather than after.

### How many saturation plugins do I need?

One broadband one you know well, and the free ones for variety. Harmonics stack across a mix, so owning six and using four at once is a faster route to a crowded record than to a warm one.

### Does saturation make things louder?

Yes, in two ways: it adds harmonics in ranges the ear is sensitive to, and it rounds peaks so the average level can rise. See [saturation explained](https://gazillionindustries.com/saturation-explained/) and [crest factor](https://gazillionindustries.com/crest-factor/).

### What is the best saturation plugin for 808s?

The question is better answered by what the part needs than by a product name. An 808-style sub is close to a sine, so a phone speaker reproduces almost none of the fundamental — what reaches the listener is the harmonics you add above it. So you want two things: harmonics landing high enough to survive a small speaker, and control over how much of the original low end comes through unchanged. Anything that gives you both will work, and ours is the cheapest thing here that gives you both. Note choice changes the answer too, because the harmonic series moves with the fundamental, and a part written low needs more help than one written a fourth above it.
