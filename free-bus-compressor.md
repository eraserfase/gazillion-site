## What a bus compressor does

A bus compressor listens to the sum of a group and turns the whole group down when the sum gets loud. That is the whole difference from compressing a single track. The kick pushes the detector, the gain dips, and the hats, the room and everything else in the group dip with it for a moment and come back. Things that share a moving gain start to sound like they were played in one space.

The second effect is density. Pull the peaks down two or three decibels, bring the level back up with makeup gain, and everything that was not a peak is now louder: the snare's decay, the room, the tail of the reverb. On drums that reads as bigger. On a full mix it reads as finished, up to the point where it reads as flat. The [glue compression guide](https://gazillionindustries.com/glue-compression/) has the numbers behind both.

## How to set a mix bus compressor

- Finish the balance with the bus compressor bypassed.

- Insert the compressor on the mix bus and set the ratio to 2:1.

- Set the attack to 30 ms.

- Set the release to auto, or to one quarter note at your tempo (667 ms at 90 BPM).

- Lower the threshold until the loudest sections show 1 to 2 dB of gain reduction.

- Set the sidechain high-pass near 100 Hz if the low end is pumping the whole mix.

- Match the output to the bypassed level and compare across the loudest and quietest sections.

A quarter note is 60,000 divided by the tempo in milliseconds, so 500 ms at 120 BPM and 667 at 90. On the mix bus err longer than on drums. A release that pumps on a drum loop can be the point; on a whole song it is a fault you hear on the second listen.

## Free master bus compressor

The master is the last place to fix anything. If the mix bus is already compressed gently, the master usually needs nothing but a limiter. If you do compress the master, use the most transparent compressor you own at a decibel or less, and check it on the quietest section of the song as well as the loudest. The [mastering guide](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-master-a-beat/) and [limiter vs compressor](https://gazillionindustries.com/limiter-vs-compressor/) cover the order.

## What a bus compressor costs

Every decibel of gain reduction on a bus is a decibel of dynamics the song no longer has. Stack a drum bus compressor, a mix bus compressor and a master limiter all working hard and the chorus stops being louder than the verse. Keep each stage gentle, or let one stage do the heavy lifting and keep the others light.

A bus compressor also couples the group to its loudest member. On a mix bus the kick and the bass drive the detector, so every low note ducks the vocal a little. A sidechain high-pass is the fix, and if your compressor lacks one, compress less.

## Questions people ask

### What is a bus compressor?

A compressor placed on a group of tracks, a bus, instead of a single track. It reacts to the combined signal, so everything in the group moves together, which is where the glued, one-room sound comes from.

### What is the difference between a bus compressor and a normal compressor?

Mostly placement. Any compressor can go on a bus. Compressors sold as bus compressors are tuned to sound good at low gain reduction with slower timing, because that is how they get used on groups.

### Do you need a bus compressor?

On the drum bus, nearly always; it is the fastest way to make a kit sound like one performance, and DRUGS does it free. On the mix bus, only if the bypass comparison at matched level says so.

### What is the best free bus compressor?

### What attack and release should a bus compressor use?

On a drum bus, a 10 to 30 ms attack and a release near an eighth note. On a mix bus, a slower attack around 30 ms and a release near a quarter note, or auto. DRUGS sets both from DOSE and adapts the release to each hit.
