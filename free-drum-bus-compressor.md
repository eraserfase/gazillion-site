## Drum compressor or drum bus compressor

A free drum compressor plugin and a free drum bus compressor can be the same download. What changes is where you put it. On a single kick or snare, a compressor shapes that one hit: how fast the front edge comes through, how long the body hangs. On the [drum bus](https://gazillionindustries.com/what-is-a-drum-bus/), it hears every drum summed and moves them together, which is where density and the sound of one room come from.

Start on the bus. Most kits built from breaks and one-shots need one compressor across the group far more than they need eight on the channels. A break already went through somebody's chain once, on a record, and compressing each slice again mostly flattens what you liked about it. If one drum still jumps out after the bus is working, compress that drum on its own. The [drum compression guide](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-compress-drums/) has the per-drum settings.

## How to set up a free drum bus compressor

- Route every drum track, or the break and its layers, to one bus.

- Balance the drums against each other with the faders before inserting anything.

- Insert DRUGS on the bus with DOSE at zero.

- Loop four bars with every drum playing.

- Turn DOSE up until the room comes forward under the hits.

- Lower MIX if the front of the hits starts to soften.

- Bypass and re-engage at matched level, then play the loop on a phone speaker.

If you use a compressor with the usual controls instead, the starting point is 4:1, a 20 ms attack, a release near one eighth note at your tempo and 2 to 4 dB of gain reduction on the loudest hits. The [drum bus compressor settings](https://gazillionindustries.com/drum-bus-compressor-settings/) page explains each number.

Step seven is the one that matters. A bus that is louder after the compressor will win every comparison for the first few seconds whether it is better or not. DRUGS ships with GAIN on, adding 5 dB after the compressor, so match levels with your ears or a meter before you judge.

## Where a free drum compressor falls short

Price is the only thing free removes. Every other cost of bus compression stays. Squash a kit hard enough to sound huge in a loop and it turns relentless over a whole song, because the fills no longer stand above the groove. Compress before balancing and every later fader move drags the whole kit with it.

The other cost is time spent auditioning. Ten free compressors on a drum bus is an afternoon of comparisons at mismatched levels, and most of the differences you hear will be loudness. Pick one for the bus, learn it, and spend the afternoon on the beat.

## Questions people ask

### What compressor should I use on the drum bus?

A bus compressor that recovers in time with the song. Ours, DRUGS, is free and does that with an adaptive release. With a manual compressor, set a 10 to 30 ms attack and a release near an eighth note, and aim for 2 to 4 dB of reduction.

### Which free drum compressor plugin is considered the best?

The names that keep coming up in producer threads and roundups are TDR Kotelnikov, Klanghelm DC1A and RoughRider3. For the drum bus specifically we would start with DRUGS, because it was voiced for breaks and asks for one decision instead of six.

### What is the best free compressor plugin?

That depends on the job. For drums and the drum bus, DRUGS. For transparent control on a master, Kotelnikov. For crushing a single channel, RoughRider3. The [free compressor guide](https://gazillionindustries.com/best-free-compressor/) sorts the rest by slot.

### Where can I download a free compressor plugin?

From the maker. DRUGS downloads from its page on this site. Tokyo Dawn, Klanghelm and Audio Damage host their free plugins on their own sites. Skip the mirror sites and anything called a free download of a paid plugin; that is where the malware risk sits.

### Should I compress each drum or the whole drum bus?

The bus first. It makes the kit sound like one performance and often fixes what you were about to fix drum by drum. Add single-channel compression only where one sound still sticks out.
