## The three jobs a hip hop synth has to do

Sort the job before you shop. Almost every synth sound in rap, trap, drill and boom bap falls into one of three roles, and the instrument that serves one of them well is often mediocre at the others. Knowing which one you are hiring for cuts the field by about two thirds before you download anything.

**Bass.** One note at a time, low, loud, and carrying the harmony on its own. This is the role that decides whether the beat works in a car, and it is the one people most often try to fill with a preset built for a different genre. It needs a clean low fundamental, a filter you can park low, and an envelope that lets go before the next kick.

**Leads and hooks.** Also one note at a time, usually in a narrow window between 1 kHz and 4 kHz where nothing else in the beat lives. Bells, flutes, plucked synths, the melody that gets stuck in someone's head. Here the sound has to be distinctive within about three notes, because that is all a hook gets.

**Keys, pads and texture.** Chords under the top line, held and filtered back so they read as air rather than as parts. This is the only one of the three that genuinely wants polyphony, and it is also the one a sample will often do better than a synth will.

Two of the three roles are monophonic by nature. That is the whole reason the answer to "best synth plugins for hip hop" keeps coming back to a one-voice instrument instead of the biggest library on the market.

## Why the best synth plugins for hip hop are mostly one voice

A monosynth plays one note at a time and puts everything it has behind that note. Every oscillator, the whole filter, the whole envelope, one pitch. When you play a second key before releasing the first, the instrument does not start a new voice; it slides the one voice it has to the new pitch, and that slide is a musical event rather than a limitation.

The practical consequence is level. Three oscillators stacked on one note, detuned a few cents apart, produce a single fat pitch with a slow beat in it. Split those same three oscillators across a three-note chord and you have one thin oscillator per note, three pitches fighting for the same low register, and a low end that turns to mud the moment the kick lands.

The second consequence is glide. Hand a monosynth two overlapping notes and it travels between them. A polysynth starts a second voice and leaves the first to decay. Every sliding bass figure in trap, drill and modern rap depends on that first behavior, which is why producers who have never used a hardware synth still end up wanting a monophonic one.

There is a third consequence and it is unglamorous: fewer voices means less to go wrong. One set of controls, one signal path, one thing to hear. When a patch is wrong you can find the wrong part, because there is only one of everything.

## What the controls do, in the order you touch them

Subtractive synthesis is a short list of stages in a fixed order, and every synth in this article runs some version of it. Oscillators make a raw tone rich in harmonics. A mixer sets how much of each one you get. A filter removes part of that spectrum. Envelopes decide when the filter and the volume move. Everything else is modulation on top.

Top left makes the sound, top right takes things away from it. Under the blue headers is modulation; under the cream ones is how it gets played.

**Oscillators and octave.** Pitch is set twice: once by the key you press and once by the octave switch, marked in feet on JOURNEYMAN's three oscillators. Play C2 at 8' and the fundamental is 65.41 Hz. The same key at 16' is 32.70 Hz, and at 32' it is 16.35 Hz, which is under the bottom of human hearing and under the bottom of every consumer speaker ever sold. The octave switch is where most weak bass patches are lost, before a single filter is touched.

**Wave.** A triangle is nearly pure and gives you a fundamental with very little above it. A saw contains every harmonic. A square contains only the odd ones, which is why it reads as hollow and woody. A narrow pulse is thin and nasal and cuts through a busy mix at low level. JOURNEYMAN puts that whole range on one continuous WAVE knob per oscillator, from triangle through saw and square to pulse, so you can sweep it and stop where the note sounds right instead of picking from a list.

**Detune.** Two oscillators at slightly different pitches beat against each other at the difference frequency. Offset them by 8 cents and two oscillators at 110 Hz drift apart by about 0.51 Hz, one slow swell roughly every two seconds. That single number is most of what people mean by a synth sounding fat. On JOURNEYMAN the FREQUENCY knobs on oscillators two and three do this against oscillator one.

**Filter.** A lowpass filter passes what is below the cutoff and attenuates what is above it, at a slope set by how many poles it has. A pole is 6 dB per octave in the textbook sense, so a three-pole and a four-pole setting land an octave above cutoff 6 dB apart, which is half the amplitude. JOURNEYMAN has two ladder filters, one per channel, with a shared CUTOFF, a SPACING control that offsets one from the other, RESONANCE, and a poles switch for each. The long version of what cutoff and resonance do lives in [filter cutoff and resonance explained](https://gazillionindustries.com/filter-cutoff-and-resonance/).

**Envelopes.** Attack, decay, sustain and release, twice: one set moving the filter, one set moving the volume. In beat production the filter envelope is doing most of the character work. A short decay with the amount turned up gives you the click and thump that lets a bass note read as percussive rather than as a held tone.

## Best synth VST for trap: glide, filter and the low octave

Trap asks a synth for two specific things. The first is pitch that travels between notes instead of jumping. The second is a low register that survives being played back on hardware designed for podcasts.

Glide is the easy half. Turn it on, set the rate, play overlapping notes, and the pitch ramps from one to the next. The rate is the whole performance: too fast and it is a blip, too slow and the note arrives late over the bar line. JOURNEYMAN puts GLIDE on a switch with GLIDE RATE beside it, and the trigger mode matters as much as the rate, because single trigger is what lets a legato phrase slide without retriggering the envelope on every key.

The low register is the hard half. A phone speaker is a few millimeters across and produces almost nothing under a few hundred hertz; a laptop is not much better. A note at 32.70 Hz is inaudible on both, but its harmonics at 65.4, 98.1 and 130.8 Hz are not, and those harmonics are what tell a listener the pitch. So you write the part low and then make sure the harmonics exist, either by choosing a wave that already has them or by adding them afterwards.

Adding them afterwards is a saturation job rather than a synthesis job, and it is covered properly in [how to make bass audible on phone speakers](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-bass-audible-on-phone-speakers/) and [how to distort 808s without losing the low end](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-distort-808s/). The same arithmetic applies to a synth bass and to a sampled one.

Pitch and time on the sub itself is a third thing again, and for slides that bend the whole sample rather than the note, see [how to pitch a sample](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-pitch-a-sample/).

## Build a bass patch: the steps

Do this once from scratch and the rest of the instrument stops being mysterious. Values below are starting points on JOURNEYMAN's 0 to 10 knobs; the same sequence works on any subtractive synth with the controls renamed.

- Press INIT to start from a blank program.

- In the MIXER, switch OSC 1 on and set it to 10. Leave OSC 2, OSC 3, NOISE and EXT IN off.

- Set OSC 1 OCTAVE to 16'.

- Set OSC 1 WAVE to 0 for a triangle, then raise it to about 3 for a saw.

- Switch OSC 2 on in the MIXER at 8 and set its OCTAVE to 16'.

- Turn OSC 2 FREQUENCY until you hear one slow beat about every two seconds.

- Set the filter switch to DUAL LP and set both FILTER A POLES and FILTER B POLES to 4 POLE.

- Set CUTOFF to 2, RESONANCE to 0 and SPACING to center.

- Set VOLUME ENV to ATTACK 0, DECAY 4, SUSTAIN 8, RELEASE 1.

- Set FILTER ENV to ATTACK 0, DECAY 3, SUSTAIN 0, RELEASE 1, and AMOUNT to about 3.

- Set KB AMT to 5 so the filter opens as you play up the keyboard.

- Set KEYBOARD to LAST, TRIGGER to SINGLE, switch GLIDE on and set GLIDE RATE to 2.

- Play the part and watch the OVERLOAD lamp. If it lights, lower the MIXER channels rather than MASTER.

- Bounce eight bars and play them back on a phone before you change anything.

The last step is the one people skip, and it is the only one that tells you the truth. The patch that sounded enormous in headphones at step twelve is the same patch that disappeared at three in the morning by the front door.

## Leads, keys and the rest of the 200 programs

A lead patch is the bass patch with three things moved. Take the octave up to 8' or 4', open the cutoff until the tone is bright enough to hear against the drums, and shorten the volume decay so notes stop rather than smear. Add resonance last, in small amounts, because resonance near the cutoff is the fastest way to make a synth sound cheap.

Keys and pads are the awkward case on a monosynth, and the honest answer is to play them as parts rather than as chords: a root line and a separate top line, recorded twice, panned apart. That is more work than holding a triad, and it also sounds less like a preset. Producers who work this way have been doing it for thirty years, which is part of why the beats in [Dilla type beats](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-dilla-type-beats/) and [Madlib type beats](https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-madlib-type-beats/) have the harmonic looseness they do.

The touch surface is the part people use longest. It turns a static patch into a performance without automation lanes, and it records as automation if you want it to.

## What a monosynth costs you

One voice means one voice. You cannot hold a chord, you cannot let a note ring under the next one, and you cannot build a pad by stacking fifths in a single instance. Every chord becomes a second track, and every track is another fader, another bounce and another decision. On a busy session that adds up.

You also give up the preset economy. A wavetable synth with 1,700 presets is a browsing instrument; you scroll until something is interesting and then write to it. A monosynth with 200 programs is a playing instrument, and if you work by browsing, the smaller list will feel thin for the first week.

And there is no sample engine here. If the sound you need is a rhodes, a flute or a vinyl string stab, this is the wrong tool and a sampler is the right one. A synth that claims to do everything usually does the thing you need adequately and nothing memorably.

## After the synth: getting it into the beat

A synth part almost never sits right raw. Three moves fix most of it, in this order.

First, level and headroom. Synths run hot and a patch with three oscillators open can be 10 dB louder than the sampled parts around it. Set it before you process it; the reasoning is in [gain staging](https://gazillionindustries.com/gain-staging/).

Second, harmonics. A synth bass that vanishes on small speakers needs content above the fundamental, and saturation is how you get it. Third, the drums around it. If the synth and the kick are arguing, the fix is usually on the drum bus rather than on the synth. ### What are the best synth plugins for hip hop if I only buy one?

Buy for the role you fill most often. If that is bass and hooks, which for most beatmakers it is, one monophonic synth covers more of your week than a large library will. If you make beats that live on held chords and evolving texture, buy the wavetable instrument instead and accept that the bass will take an extra step.

Vital's Basic tier, which its own page lists as the full synth with all features, 75 presets and 25 wavetables at no cost. Surge XT is the other one, open source and free, with over 2,800 factory patches. Before either, check the instrument your DAW already installed. Free will get you a long way on leads and textures, and a little less far on a played monophonic bass.

### What is the best synth VST for trap?

The one with glide on the front panel and a trigger mode that does not restart the envelope mid-phrase. Sliding pitch between overlapping notes is the defining gesture of the style, and a polyphonic synth answers those overlapping notes with new voices instead. JOURNEYMAN has GLIDE with its own rate control, selectable note priority and single or multi trigger, which is the combination that makes slides play cleanly.

### What are the best synth plugins for trap bass specifically?

For a synthesized trap bass you want a low octave, a triangle or saw, a lowpass parked low, a short filter decay for the thump and glide for the slides. That is a small feature list and almost any monosynth has it; what differs is how quickly you can get there. After the synth, the bass needs harmonics to survive small speakers, which is a saturation step rather than a synth setting.

### Do you need a polyphonic synth to make beats?

No, and most producers use polyphony less than they expect. Bass is monophonic, hooks are monophonic, and chords are frequently sampled rather than played. When you do want a pad, record the monosynth twice as two lines and pan them apart; you get a wider result than a triad in one instance, at the cost of one extra pass.

### Is Serum worth it for hip hop?

For wavetable leads, screaming risers and modern sound design, yes, and its own page prices Serum 2 at $249 with lifetime free updates. For a bass line, you are buying five oscillator types to use one. Plenty of people own both a large wavetable synth and a small monophonic one, and use the small one more days of the week.

### Where should you download synth plugins?

From the maker's own site, always. These searches land on aggregator pages that reprint the developer's blurb word for word, wrap it in a download button, and sit years behind the current version. Read about a synth wherever you find it, then go to the developer and get the file there.
