# RC-20 Retro Color

> What RC-20 Retro Color actually does: the six modules in order, why it is a series chain, what Magnitude controls, and what reviewers say it is not built for.

Six boxes in a row, one big knob across the top, and a preset list full of words like "cassette" and "old radio". It is the plugin most people reach for first when they want a clean recording to stop sounding clean, and it is worth understanding rather than guessing at.

RC-20 Retro Color is a character effect by XLN Audio, announced in December 2016. It chains six modules in series, left to right: NOISE, WOBBLE, DISTORT, DIGITAL, SPACE and MAGNETIC. One global control, Magnitude, scales the depth of everything at once. There is no global dry/wet: the mix controls live inside the individual modules. Everything below comes from the maker's own manual and from published reviews.

## The short version

- **Maker**: XLN Audio; announced December 2016

- **Modules**: NOISE, WOBBLE, DISTORT, DIGITAL, SPACE, MAGNETIC

- **Signal path**: series, left to right through all six

- **Global control**: Magnitude, which scales the whole effect

- **Mix**: per module, in WOBBLE, DISTORT and DIGITAL

- **Price**: $99, with documented sales around half that

## What each module does

- NOISE adds a noise bed from a library of recorded sources, which is what supplies the vinyl, tape and room textures.

- WOBBLE is the pitch instability: wow and flutter, the slow drift and the faster waver of a tape transport.

- DISTORT is saturation and distortion, with its own mix control.

- DIGITAL is the degrader: bit depth and sample rate reduction, with its own mix.

- SPACE is a reverb.

- MAGNETIC produces volume drops, the dropouts of a worn tape.

XLN's marketing names them slightly differently from the panel labels — Noise Generator, Wobble & Flutter, Saturation & Distortion, Degrader & Bitcrusher, Reverb, Volume Drops — which is worth knowing if you are reading about it rather than looking at it.

## Two things worth getting right

It is a series chain, not a parallel bank. The manual describes audio passing from left to right through the modules, so DISTORT is working on a signal that already has noise in it, and DIGITAL is degrading what the distortion produced. At least one review describes it as parallel. The manual is the authority, and the ordering explains why turning things up in different orders gives you different results.

Magnitude is a depth control, not a mix. It scales the effect of the whole chain, which is why pulling it back is not the same as reducing a dry/wet balance: the individual modules have their own mix knobs and those behave independently.

F(ilter)12 is built the other way round: a filter with a lo-fi section in front of it, rather than a rack of character modules in a row.

## What reviewers said it is not good at

Published criticism, attributed rather than invented. MusicRadar's only real complaint was that the SPACE module seemed superfluous, alongside a user-interface gripe that bypassed modules disappear from view. Tape Op described it as "not a clinical, surgical, X-Acto knife of a plugin" and noted the absence of auto-gain, which matters because adding noise and distortion changes the level and you end up compensating by hand. Another reviewer wanted a tape delay, tempo sync and a soft-clip limiter added.

None of those are damning. They describe a tool built for fast character rather than precise surgery, which is what it is sold as.

## How it compares to what we make

They overlap in one module and diverge everywhere else, so it is worth being precise instead of competitive about it.

The overlap is degradation. RC-20's DIGITAL module reduces bit depth and sample rate. F(ilter)12's lo-fi section does the same two things with SAMPLE RATE and BIT, and the page is specific about the mechanism: lowering the rate means the top goes and what was up there folds back down as new content, while coarsening the bit depth rounds off quiet detail and puts a hard edge on what is left.

The divergence is the filter. RC-20 is a chain of character processors with no filter section at all. F(ilter)12 is primarily a filter: five shapes, a slope that runs from 18 dB per octave up to a wall, resonance and Q as separate controls, six resonance shapes that travel with the cutoff, and two ways of moving the cutoff automatically. It also carries an adaptive correction called AURA that RC-20 has no equivalent for, and RC-20 carries noise, wobble, reverb and dropouts that F(ilter)12 has no equivalent for.

So: one is for making a clean recording sound like it came off a worn medium. The other is for moving a filter through a sound and driving it. Plenty of people will want both, and neither one replaces the other.

## Questions people ask

### What is RC-20 Retro Color?

A character plugin by XLN Audio that chains six modules — noise, wobble, distortion, digital degradation, reverb and volume drops — to make recordings sound older or more worn, with a single Magnitude control scaling the whole effect.

### How much does RC-20 cost?

$99 at list, with a subscription option, and it has been discounted to around half that in documented sales.

### Does RC-20 have a dry/wet control?

Not globally. Magnitude scales the depth of the whole chain, and individual mix controls sit inside the WOBBLE, DISTORT and DIGITAL modules.

### Is RC-20 in series or parallel?

Series. The manual describes the signal passing from left to right through the six modules, so each one processes what the previous one produced.

## What F(ilter)12 does

F(ilter)12 is a filter with a lo-fi section in front of it and an adaptive correction called AURA on top. SAMPLE RATE and BIT are the crush pair. POSITION sets where in the spectrum the filter sits, and the further it works the more it drives and the more it sings. There are five shapes — low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch and a morph — and SLOPE runs from 18 dB per octave to a wall. Q SHAPE puts peaks and dips at fixed distances from the cutoff so the whole shape travels with the knob: WAH, VOWEL, COMB, SCOOP, PLAIN, or DRAW your own.

Six boxes in a row is one design. A filter you can drive and move is another. Know which problem you have.
